Balance Sheet Cleanup: Findings & Proposed Corrections

Prepared for Balanced Ledgers (CPA) From Adan Gonzales, Volition Components Books: Odoo (converted from QuickBooks, Dec 2023)

Bottom line: the business is financially sound. Cash, receivables, payables, inventory, fixed assets, loans, and owner equity all reconcile to the prior QuickBooks records. Nothing here suggests missing funds.

Nearly every issue traces to one root cause: the December 2023 conversion from QuickBooks to Odoo was handled by a former contract bookkeeper who used a non-standard method. He recorded prior-period customer receipts directly into equity (retained earnings) rather than through income or accounts receivable. We have traced each item to its source journal entry and propose corrections below.

This memo is written in plain accounting terms. No Odoo knowledge is required. We have detailed line-item working papers available if you want to see any entry in full.

What we need from you

These are the decisions that require CPA judgment because they touch a prior year or a filed return. Our bookkeeper will make the entries once you confirm the treatment.

  1. Three 2023 customer deposits on jobs finished in 2024 — recognize as customer deposits deferred into 2024, or leave as prior-period equity? (Affects 2023 vs. 2024 income.)
  2. One unidentified $10,891.54 deposit — once we trace the check, is it 2023 income (equity) or 2024 income? (If 2024, current income is understated by that amount.)
  3. 2023 migration keying errors (operating bank and clearing accounts) — correct as prior-period adjustments, or current-dated reclassifications?
  4. 2023 payroll-tax balances that carried over from QuickBooks and were never reconciled — how should these be cleared against the filed 941s and state returns you hold?
  5. Consolidating two retained-earnings accounts into one — confirm approach and opening balance.
Root cause, in one paragraph

When the books moved from QuickBooks to Odoo at the end of 2023, several open items (customer deposits on unfinished jobs, checks received right at the cutover, and opening balances) had to be carried across. The former bookkeeper's approach was to book these prior-period receipts straight into retained earnings as, in his words, “income we earned… to increase our investment.” That is why a manually-migrated retained-earnings account became a catch-all, and why a few clearing accounts carry migration “plug” balances. The underlying transactions are real; the classification is what needs correcting.
