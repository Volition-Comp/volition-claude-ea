# Municipal Fleet Sales Call Plan

_For Adan + Tony. Built 2026-06-26. Pairs with the [lead sheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1x8cYizqF1i9suM3urCUKpvX3q8DnlfcTnv5hduElMao/edit) and the [call list](municipal-fleet-call-list.md)._

## The goal
Open conversations with Front Range public fleets and turn them into multi-vehicle upfit jobs. This feeds Adan's 20 fleet sales target. Municipalities buy in batches (a run of new vans for parks, utilities, facilities, school transport), which is exactly the multi-vehicle work we want.

## Why they'd take the call
Cities, counties, and school districts run fleets of cargo and service vans that need shelving, racks, partitions, refrigeration, and specialty builds. They replace and add vehicles on a budget cycle. When a new van shows up, someone has to outfit it. That's us. We're a local Colorado upfitter, so we're faster and easier to work with than a national outfit, and they can come see the shop.

## Who to ask for
Priority order, by who actually owns the buy:
1. **Fleet Manager / Fleet Superintendent / Fleet Services Manager** (cities + counties). Owns the vehicles and the upfit decision.
2. **Director of Transportation / Transportation Director** (school districts). Owns the bus and service-vehicle fleet.
3. **Director of Facilities & Fleet Management** (some counties combine these).
4. **Public Works Director** (smaller towns with no dedicated fleet manager). The PW director or their shop foreman makes the call.

The sheet tags everyone P1 (decision-maker), P2 (assistant/supervisor, good backdoor), or P3 (public works leadership, use when there's no fleet contact). **Start with P1s.**

## Call flow

**Opening (keep it short):**
"Hi [Name], this is [Adan/Tony] with Volition Components. We're a Colorado van upfitter over in [city]. We build out work vans, shelving, ladder racks, refrigeration, the specialty stuff. I'm calling fleet folks around the Front Range because we'd like to be on your list when you're outfitting new vehicles. Do you handle the upfit side of the fleet, or is that someone else?"

If it's not them, ask who and get a name + extension.

**Qualify (2 to 4 questions):**
- "Roughly how many vans or service vehicles do you run, and how many do you cycle through in a year?"
- "When you add a new van, who does the shelving and upfit now? In-house or a vendor?"
- "Anything you wish was different about how those builds get done? Lead time, fit, cost?"
- "Do you ever deal with refrigerated or specialty units? Command, medical, anything like that?"

**Position (match to what they said):**
- High volume, uses a vendor → "We can probably beat their lead time and we're local, so you're not shipping vehicles out of state."
- In-house → "Totally fine. We're good as overflow when your shop is slammed or for the builds that take special equipment."
- Refrigeration / specialty → lean in hard, that's our differentiator.

**Close for the next step (not the sale):**
- "Can I send you a one-pager and some photos of recent builds so you've got us on file?" (get/confirm email)
- "Would it be worth a 20-minute visit so I can see your typical van and show you what we'd do?"
- "When's your next budget cycle for vehicle replacements? I'd like to time a follow-up to that."

## Common objections
- **"We already have a vendor."** → "Makes sense. I'm not asking you to switch. Keep us as a backup quote so you've got leverage and coverage when they're backed up."
- **"We do it in-house."** → "Smart. We work with a lot of shops as overflow for the busy season or the odd specialty build. Mind if I leave our info?"
- **"We have to bid everything out / procurement rules."** → "Understood. Can you point me to where your bids get posted, and add us to your vendor list so we get notified?" (This is gold. Get on the bid list.)
- **"No budget right now."** → "No problem. When does the next cycle open? I'll check back then." Log the date.

## Cadence (per contact)
1. Call (or call + email if no answer).
2. No answer → voicemail + email same day with the one-pager.
3. No reply in 4 to 5 business days → second call.
4. Still nothing → email again in ~2 weeks, then park it and revisit next quarter.
Log every touch in the sheet (Call Status, Date Contacted, Notes). Mark Lead Quality Good / Bad / Maybe as you learn.

## Suggested weekly rhythm
- Block 2 calling sessions a week, ~10 to 15 dials each. Tony and Adan split the list by geography or by city vs school district.
- Work P1s first, top to bottom. Counties and big metro cities (Denver, Aurora, Arapahoe, Adams, Douglas, El Paso, Jeffco) have the biggest fleets, so they're worth the most prep.
- Friday: 10 minutes to update the sheet and tee up next week's follow-ups.
